# Life Plan

> **Mission:** Live, Learn, Grow.

## How to use this document

This is the **stable, high-level source of direction**, not a comprehensive to-do list. It preserves the mission, values, and life categories from the original *Planning Overview*. Annual plans translate this direction into a few meaningful outcomes; six-week reviews inspect progress and make adjustments; weekly and daily task tools carry out the work.

**Review cadence**

| Interval | Primary question | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual | Where should my life be heading, and what matters this year? | `Annual/2027.md` (when created) |
| Every six weeks | Am I progressing in the right areas, and what will I focus on next? | `Reviews/YYYY-MM-DD.md` |
| Weekly | What are this week's most important outcomes and commitments? | Task/calendar tools; optional weekly note |
| Daily | What deserves my attention today? | Existing task/calendar tools |

> **Drafting principle:** The mission and Code of Conduct below are retained from the source. New operating rules and review questions are *proposals*, not assertions about established personal commitments.

## 1. Life objective and mission

### Live, Learn, Grow

I want to learn everything that I can, in many areas of my life. I will never pass up an opportunity to learn. One important step in the learning process is experience, live what you learn and learn what you live. Ultimately, the learning process will make you grow. I strive to live these principles and apply them into all areas of my life. I try to understand the problems I face by learning as much as I can about them. I apply that knowledge and try to understand the proper application of the knowledge I have learned. I try to learn as an individual and as a team member. Growth occurs as I gain experience. I try grow a little each day, by gaining knowledge and experience. The growth I strive for encompasses breadth and depth, which I feel is necessary for one to be balanced.

*Source: Planning Overview, page 1; original wording preserved.*

## 2. Code of Conduct

*Original wording preserved from Planning Overview, page 2.*

> If you are going to ride, then you might as well be the best. In order to achieve the excellence that being the best entails, I will live by the following code of conduct:

1. I will be disciplined in whatever I pursue, because discipline equals freedom. (Alma 41:20)
2. I will always act with courage and bravery whenever faced with difficult decisions and choices. (D&C 128:22)
3. I know it is my responsibility to protect, provide, and preside; and I will act accordingly. (3 Nephi 27:13)
4. I understand that if I am to achieve my goals, I must work hard with the entirety of my heart, might, mind, and strength. (2 Peter 1:5-10)
5. I will strive to keep myself spiritually enlightened, physically strong, mentally awake, and morally straight. (Luke 2:52)
6. I take responsibility for my thoughts, choices, and actions; and will learn from my mistakes. (D&C 101:78)
7. I respect myself, others, and my surroundings, even when that respect is not reciprocated. (D&C 134:6)
8. I believe in being honest and truthful in every way, especially when it is hard. (D&C 82:10, Luke 22:42)
9. I think with my mind, feel with my heart, and follow my soul. (2 Nephi 2:26)
10. I know that Jesus Christ is my Savior, and that through his Atonement I can overcome all things. (Alma 42:23)

> As I live according to this code of conduct, I make no excuses and have no regrets.

### Annual direction

Annual planning is the primary bridge from long-term vision to execution. Suggested output:

1. Review the mission, principles, life-area definitions, and 5-/10-year direction.
2. Evaluate the past year, including family, faith, work, health, finances, service, recreation, and learning.
3. Identify the year's **few highest-priority outcomes**; not every area needs a major project every year.
4. Decide what routines and responsibilities must be maintained even without an annual goal.
5. Calendar immovable commitments and protected time: birthdays, anniversaries, holidays, school breaks, travel, appointments, community events, and planning sessions.
6. Set approximate six-week review dates and record the first cycle's focus.

**Annual plan:** `Annual/2027.md` (placeholder; use the applicable year when ready).

### Six-week direction

At the end of each cycle, review each life area, record the major wins and tensions, and commit to **no more than three focus outcomes across the entire cycle** as a starting constraint. These are separate from ongoing duties and habits. Use [the six-week template](Templates/Six-Week-Review.md).

### Weekly and daily execution

Keep calendars, task lists, schedules, and detailed habit tracking outside this core plan. Weekly planning should check current six-week outcomes and choose the week's meaningful actions. Daily planning should remain light enough to use on disrupted days.

## 5. Duties, habits, and supporting systems

A responsibility is not necessarily a project goal. Duties such as family commitments, employment, service, and stewardship may recur indefinitely. For each important duty, capture its purpose, acceptable time commitment, and related life area in a future `Duties.md`.

The original PDF also contains SAVERS, a detailed daily After Action Review, a Perfect Week schedule, a GTD-style trigger list, a goals sheet, and a weekly scripture-study schedule. Retain these in a **reference archive**, not as mandatory sections of this core plan.

### Reference week — proposed rename

Treat the original **Perfect Week** (pages 14–18) as a reference for an ordinary, low-disruption week, not a standard for judging all weeks. Adjust for actual family obligations, energy, sleep, and changes in season.
